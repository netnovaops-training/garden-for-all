# Using Jun's Garden

A picture for every step. The pictures come from a test copy of the garden with made-up sample
plants, so your plants and names will look different. Big picture = computer, small picture =
phone. The thing to look at has an orange frame.

This is the whole manual: every page of the website, every tab of the admin page, the phone app and
the garden computer. Read the parts you need; the contents below link to each one. Two parts at the
end are still being built and say so.

## 1. Signing in, and your password {.part}

Anyone can look at the garden. To add or change plants, sign in.

1. Open your garden's web address. The home page has a login box: type your name (**Jun**) and your
   password and press **Login**. (The same box is on its own page, `/login.php`.)
   @shot 01-login
2. Signed in, the home page says **Welcome back, Jun!**, and the admin pages open for you.
   @shot 03-home-signed-in
3. **Forgot your password, or the first time?** On the login page choose **Forgot password? / First
   time?**. Type your name; a link to set a new password goes to your email.
   @shot 02-forgot

*On the garden computer you can also set it any time: `bash garden.sh password` (or the menu, U, then 5).*

**Signing out:** the **Logout** button is on the home page, at the top right of the Full Catalog and in
the admin page's left column.

## 4. The Full Catalog {.part}

**Full Catalog** is the heart of the garden: every plant as a card, with search, filters and
colours. Everyone can look; signed in, you also get the owner's buttons.

### 4.1 What you see first

1. **Visitors** see a login box at the top (the same login as on the home page). Signed in, that place
   holds the **🪴 Add / Edit Plant** box instead (section 4.13).
   @shot 25-catalog-login
2. Under it, a green pill counts your garden: **You have N plants (across M varieties)**. "Plants"
   counts every living plant by its quantity; "varieties" counts the different plant records. Dead and
   removed plants are not counted.
3. Then come, in this order: the **search box**, **A–Z Plants**, **Quick Search** (drop-downs),
   **Views** (signed in only), **Tags**, **Bed Search**, the **Card Theme** box, and the cards.

### 4.2 The search box and its suggestions

1. Type in **Search plants...** and press **Enter**: the cards change to the plants that match.
   @shot 28-catalog-search-box
2. After two letters a list of **suggestions** opens: 🌿 a plant, 🍃 a plant family, 🏪 a brand or
   store, ⭐ a wish-list plant (marked **WISH LIST**). Click one, or move with the arrow keys and press
   **Enter**. **Esc** closes the list.
   @shot 29-catalog-suggestions
3. To see every plant again, empty the box and press **Enter**.

Chapter 5 says exactly what the search looks in, with tips.

### 4.3 A–Z and the Quick Search drop-downs

1. **A–Z Plants:** buttons such as **B–G** or **L–V**. Letters with few plants are put together so each
   button holds about 20 plants. A button shows the plants whose name starts with those letters, sorted
   by name (dead and removed plants left out). Hold the pointer on a button to see how many it holds.
   @shot 30-catalog-az-quick
2. **Quick Search** has four drop-downs and a button. Each choice simply runs a search for you:
   - **🌿 Plant Families:** Coleus, Evergreens, Heuchera, Hostas, Hydrangeas, Japanese Maples, Phlox, Roses.
   - **🏪 Brands & Stores:** the growers and shops (for example Proven Winners, Monrovia, Home Depot).
   - **📅 Year Planted:** a year from 2021 to 2026.
   - **⭐ Favorites:** **⭐ Jun's Favs**, **⭐ Author's favs**, **🐾 Kona's Favs**: the plants on that
     person's favourites list (the ☆ star in a plant's window, section 4.9).
   - **💖 Community Favs:** plants that visitors and other accounts have starred.
3. After an A–Z button the cards are in name order:
   @shot 41-search-az

### 4.4 Views (signed in) and QR tags

1. **👁️ Views** (only when signed in) show plants by their review state:
   **📸 Image Approved** / **🚫 Image Not Approved** (has the photo been checked?), **✅ Jun Done** /
   **✅ Author done** (the plants that person ticked as done), and **❌ Not Done** (the plants *you*
   have not ticked yet).
   @shot 31-catalog-views-tags
2. **🏷️ Show QR Tags** shows a QR code for every living plant, right on the page. Press it again to
   hide them. (For printing, the labels page is better: chapter 11.)

### 4.5 Bed Search

1. Five groups open with a tap: **🧭 North Beds**, **🧭 South Beds**, **🧭 East Beds**, **🥗 Edibility**
   and **🗄️ Archival & Planning**. Only one group is open at a time. Tap a bed to see its plants.
   @shot 32-catalog-beds
2. **🥗 Edibility** sorts plants by what is safe to eat:
   - **🍽️ Fully Edible:** the fruit is edible and the plant is non-toxic.
   - **🥗 Semi Edible:** the fruit is edible, but the plant is toxic to dogs (eat with care).
   - **🦜 Wildlife Only:** the fruit is for birds and animals, not people.
   - **☠️ Completely Toxic:** toxic fruit, or toxic to humans.
3. **🗄️ Archival & Planning:** **💀 Dead / Removed**, **📬 To Be Sorted** (plants with no bed yet)
   and **🌠 Wishlist** (plants you want but do not have yet).

### 4.6 Card Theme, Layout and Effect

The **Card Theme** box above the cards changes how the cards look. It is remembered on that computer
or phone and, when you are signed in, for your account on every device.

1. **Card Theme:** twelve colour sets for the cards: Spring (Dark), Spring (Light), Zenith, Cherry
   (Blossom), Sunset, Dirt, Sunrise, Jun, Winter (Dark), Kona, Lavender (Light), Author. (The 🎨 button
   of chapter 2 colours the rest of the site; the two are chosen separately.)
2. **Layout:** one, two, three or four cards side by side. On a phone the cards are always one under
   the other.
3. **Effect:** a little animal or weather that follows the pointer over the cards, just for fun:
   none, deer, rabbit, bees, butterflies, drought, fire, flood, leaves, fireworks.
   @shot 33-catalog-card-theme

### 4.7 The order of the cards

- Without a search, cards are **grouped by bed** (a green bed name above each group), beds in
  alphabetical order, and inside each bed **by plant name**.
- A plant that grows in **several beds** has a card in each bed. While you search, it has one card.
- The **A–Z** buttons sort by plant name only.
- **Wish-list plants** that match a search come last, with a **Wish List** badge.
- More cards load by themselves as you scroll (nine at a time on a computer, three on a phone). At the
  very end the page says **End of Garden**.

### 4.14 The Source link

*This part comes with the "Source link" update, which is being reviewed now. Until it is in your
garden, the link does not show.*

1. A plant can have a **Source**: the web page where its grower or shop sells it. When it has one, a
   link named **Source** (always in English, also on Korean pages) appears under **By** the company, on
   the card and in the plant window. It opens the grower's page in a new tab.
2. You add it in the admin page's **✍️ Manual Editor**, in the box **Source** next to **Company /

3. Only a full web address that starts with `https://` is kept, for example
   `https://www.example.com/plant`: no spaces, at most 500 letters. Anything else is refused with a
   message and nothing is saved, so copy the address from the browser's address bar.

### 4.15 What the facts mean

| Fact | What it means |
|---|---|
| Qty | How many of this plant you have now. Changes are logged with your name. |
| Planted / Year(s) added | The year or years it went into the garden. |
| Type | Perennial, annual, biennial, shrub, tree, vine, herb, vegetable, succulent ... |
| Growth rate | How fast it grows (slow, medium, fast). |
| Zones | The USDA hardiness zones where it survives the winter, for example 4-9. |
| Sun | Full sun, part shade, full shade, or a range. |
| Water | How much water it needs (low, average, high). |
| Store / By (company) | Where it was bought / who grew or sells it. |
| Native status | Native to your area, or a cultivar (bred by people). |
| Leaf, Foliage color | Leaf type, shape and edge; the colour of the leaves. |
| Bloom | Flower colour and shape. "Not ornamental": small flowers you would not grow it for. |
| Fruit / Edibility | Fruit type and colour; who can eat it (edible, wildlife only, toxic). |
| Size, Spacing, Planting depth | Mature height × width; how far apart to plant; how deep. |
| Chilling hours | Hours of winter cold some fruit plants need before they flower. |
| Life expectancy | How many years it usually lives. |
| Avg. cost | The average price paid. |
| Root system | The kind of roots, their shape and what they do. |
| Soil type, Soil pH | The soil it likes; how acid or alkaline. |
| Attracts | The wildlife it brings: bees, butterflies, hummingbirds, birds. |
| Deer | How well it resists deer (and rabbits). |
| Toxicity | Who it is poisonous to: humans, dogs, cats, or non-toxic. |

| Notes | Care, maintenance, cleanup, propagation, companions, pests and disease, more. |

## 5. Search, on every page

### 5.1 Quick Search and the Full Catalog

1. **Quick Search**: type part of a name; matching plants appear as you type.
   @shot 16-quick-search
2. **Full Catalog**: the search box at the top filters the cards; the letters and the bed buttons
   narrow it down too.
   @shot 17-catalog-search

### 5.2 What the Full Catalog's search looks in

The search box looks for what you typed **anywhere** in these facts of every plant: the common name,
the botanical name, family, species, bed, company, store, sun, water, zones, deer resistance, toxicity,
soil type and pH, bloom and foliage colour, size and spacing, the seasons and months, the year planted,
fertilizer, cuttings, propagation, disease, and the care, maintenance and cleanup notes. Wish-list
plants are found by their name, botanical name and company.

For example, **Bees** finds the plants that attract bees:

@shot 40-search-results

**Tips**

- Capitals do not matter: `rose`, `Rose` and `ROSE` find the same plants.
- Part of a word works: `hyd` finds hydrangeas. The letters you type are looked for together, so
  `red rose` only finds a fact where exactly those words stand side by side.
- Punctuation in names is ignored: `Lil Kim` finds **Lil' Kim**.
- **Attracts** needs the whole word as it is written: `Bees` or `Hummingbirds`, not `Bee`.
- A year finds the plants planted that year: `2024`.
- A bed, a store or a brand works too: `Front Border`, `Home Depot`, `Proven Winners`.
- **Korean names:** plant names are kept in English (and Latin), so search with the English or the
  botanical name. A Korean word only finds a plant when that word is written in one of its notes.
- Nothing found? Try fewer letters, or the botanical name.

**Special searches** (the buttons type these for you; you can type them too):

| Type this | It shows |
|---|---|
| `^A` or `^B-C-D` | plants whose name starts with those letters (the A–Z buttons) |
| `edible:full`, `edible:semi`, `edible:wildlife`, `edible:toxic` | the four Edibility buttons |
| `view:image_approved`, `view:image_not_approved`, `view:jun_done`, `view:not_done` | the Views buttons (signed in) |
| `⭐ Jun's Favs`, `💖 Community Favs` | the favourites lists |

### 5.3 The Quick Search page

**Quick Search** (top bar) is a short list instead of cards: a small photo, the name, **Qty**, company,
year planted, average cost, and **📋** when the plant has a to-do note (hold the pointer on it to read
it). Type in its box: the list changes as you type (no **Enter** needed). It uses the same search as
the Full Catalog, so the tips above work here too. Wish-list plants have a purple **Wish List** mark.

## 6. Adding a plant {.part}

1. Open the tab **🌱 Add Plant**. Type the plant's common name and its botanical name.
   @shot 05-add-plant
2. Press **Enter**. The next step appears, with the button **📋 Create Record →**.
   @shot 06-add-plant-next
3. Press **📋 Create Record →**. The page checks that the plant is not in the garden already
   (**Step 2**), then asks how to add it (**Step 3**): **⚡ Quick Create** (the basics now, the rest
   later) or **✨ AI Enrich** (the free AI fills in the details). Follow the steps down the page; the
   last button saves the plant, and it appears in the catalogue.
   @shot 06b-add-plant-saved

**More about the steps**

- **📋 How to read a plant tag** (at the top, click to open it) explains which line of a nursery tag
  goes in which box.
- **Step 2 · Check for Duplicates** lists plants with a similar name. If the plant is already on your
  **wish list**, you can move that one into the garden (**🌱 Confirm: Move ... to garden database**)
  instead of making a new record. If a match is a different plant, tick **I've reviewed the matches**
  and carry on.
- **⚡ Quick Create** saves the name, botanical name and beds now; the rest can be filled in later (by
  hand in chapter 7, or with the JSON Pipeline in chapter 10).
- **✨ AI Enrich** asks the free AI to research the plant (this can take 20 to 40 seconds). You see every
  proposed value before anything is saved.
- **Start Over** clears the steps.

*A new way to add a plant with the garden's own AI, ending with an **Approve** button, is being built:
see chapter 19.*

## 7. Editing a plant

1. Open the tab **✍️ Manual Editor** and choose the plant from the list at the top.
2. Change what you want (for example the company, the bed, the sun it likes) and press **Save** at
   the bottom.
   @shot 07-edit-plant

**What the editor holds**

1. Above the form, **🌿 Related Plants in Database** lists your other plants with the same botanical
   name, and where they grow. **📄 Export current data as HTML** makes a page of the plant that you can
   print or save as a PDF.
   @shot 50-admin-editor-sections
2. The form has fourteen sections: 1 Taxonomy & Core Identity (names, family, genus, species,
   company, type, native status), 2 Growth Metrics, Spacing & Beds, 3 Microclimatic Profiles & Soil
   Requirements (sun, water, soil, zones), 4 Investment & Procurement (cost, quantity, years), 5 Bed
   Location Manager (the beds), 6 Foliage & Stem, 7 Flower, Scent & Reproduction, 8 Fruit & Berry,
   9 Root Systems, 10 Care, Hazards, Propagation & Companions, 11 Growth Calendars (the grow and bloom
   months; tick **🚫 Does not bloom** for a plant without flowers), 12 Long-Form Journals (the notes,
   and **⚠️ Flag Entry for Further Technical Review**), 13 Photos, 14 Validation Signing Authority
   (the author's and Jun's sign-off, chapter 10).
3. **Save Changes** at the bottom saves every section at once.
4. Under it, **📊 Log a Quantity Event (optional)** records a purchase, a death or a removal (chapter 8).

## 8. Removing a plant (it died, or you gave it away)

The garden keeps the plant's record and its history; you record that it is gone.

1. In **Full Catalog**, tap the plant to open its window.
   @shot 08-plant-window
2. Press **✏️ Edit**. In the green box below the form, choose the event **🗑️ Removed** (or **💀 Died**),
   how many, and save it.
   @shot 09-remove-plant

*The same box records a purchase (**🛒 Purchased / Added**) or anything else (**📝 Other**), with the
week it happened and a note. The quantity changes by itself.*

## 10. The admin page, tab by tab {.part}

All the plant tools are on one page, in tabs: **Admin** (the address ends in `/streamlit/`). Sign in
first.

1. The tabs along the top each do one job: add a plant, edit one, photos, beds, the wish list, barcodes.
   @shot 04-admin-tabs

**The left column:** who is signed in, **Logout**, the AI in use (free cloud AI) and **← Jun's
Garden** back to the website. On a phone the tabs slide sideways.

### 10.1 The AI tabs: Manual Review, Fill Missing Data, AI — Missing Only

The AI tabs use the free cloud AI that is set up on the garden computer (menu K). Without a key they
say so. **Nothing the AI suggests is saved until you approve it.**

1. **📝 Manual Review (AI):** **Search Next 3 Plants** takes three plants, asks the AI for their facts
   and shows its answers next to what you have. Keep what is right, then save.
   @shot 48-admin-ai-review
2. **✨ Fill Missing Data (AI):** **Find 3 Partially Filled Plants** finds plants with empty facts and
   lets the AI suggest them, for you to review and approve.
   @shot 49-admin-fill-missing
3. **🔒 AI — Missing Only:** the careful one. The AI may only fill facts that are completely empty; it
   never changes a fact that already has a value. One plant at a time: **🔍 Find Next Plant with Missing
   Fields**, then save or discard.
   @shot 56-admin-ai-missing

### 10.2 Checking the data: Partials, Awaiting Approval, Fully Approved, Flagged

1. **⚠️ Partials:** every plant that is still missing important facts, with the missing ones listed.

2. **🔍 Awaiting Approval:** plants whose facts are complete but that wait for the sign-off of the

### 10.3 The JSON Pipeline (many plants at once)

A JSON file is a plain text file of plant facts. The pipeline lets you take plants out, fill them in
(by hand, or with an AI on another computer) and bring them back, checking every change.

1. **📤 Phase 1 — Export:** the plants with missing facts, one at a time. Download one, then tick **Mark
   as Done** to go to the next.
   @shot 57-admin-pipeline
2. **📦 Phase 2 — Bulk Export 5:** pick up to five plants and download them as one file.
3. **🤖 Phase 2 — Batch Enrich:** the AI fills the missing facts of many plants at once (it never
   changes facts that have a value). **🌐 Include web search** and **⚡ Parallel workers** set how;
   **🚀 Start Batch Enrichment** starts it.
4. **📥 Phase 3 — Import & Review:** upload a file. Every plant is compared with what you have: new
   facts are **green**, changes to a fact that already has a value are **amber** and need your yes.
   Nothing is written until you press **Approve & Commit**.
   @shot 58-admin-pipeline-import
5. **🌟 Wishlist (Future Plants):** the same for the wish list, in three inner tabs: **➕ Add a Plant**
   (only the name is needed), **📦 Bulk Export 20** and **📥 Backfill Import & Review**. It only changes
   the wish list, never your garden's plants.
   @shot 62-add-wishlist-plant
6. **📋 Field Reference:** every fact a file can hold, its kind, the allowed values and an example.
   **🔍 Filter fields** finds one.
   @shot 59-admin-field-reference

### 10.4 Bed Assignment, Wishlist, Barcodes

1. **🗺️ Bed Assignment:** chapter 11.
2. **🌟 Wishlist:** the plants you want. **🔍 Filter by name**, pick one, and see what is missing.
   **✏️ Edit Fields** changes it, **🌱 Promote to Garden** moves it into your garden when you buy it,
   **🗑️ Remove from Wishlist** deletes it. **📋 Full Wishlist Table** shows them all.
   @shot 60-admin-wishlist
3. **🏷️ Barcodes:** looks up the barcode (UPC) on a plant's pot or tag in a free online list (100
   look-ups a day; each answer is kept, so it is asked only once). **Single Plant Lookup**: choose the

   without a barcode, **🚀 Start Batch Lookup**. The phone app's **Barcode** button uses these.
   @shot 61-admin-barcodes

**Data** turns your garden into charts.

   **%** or **Z-Score** (how unusual a number is). The small boxes under each dimension filter the table

   Detective** (pick a plant: the weather it lived through and what the AI makes of it), the **Weather

## 18. The garden computer: menu, backups and settings {.part}

Everything the website does not do is done on the garden computer, in its menu: type
`bash garden.sh` in the garden folder (on Windows: **Garden-Menu-Windows.cmd**). Type a number or a
letter and press **Enter**; **0** goes back. **L** switches the menu between English, Korean and both.
Every choice is explained in guide **19, Every menu option** (Help, chapter 15).

@menu screen-01-main-menu

### 18.1 Every day

- **2 / 3:** start or stop the garden. **4:** open the website.
- **5) Checklist: is everything OK?** checks every part and says what to do when something is wrong.
- After a power cut or a restart the garden starts by itself (guide 25, Common questions).
- **M, then 14** (on / off) has the automatic check-up: when it is on, every 6 hours it starts again any
  program that got stuck.

### 18.2 Backups (menu 6)

Every backup is one `.tar` file in the `backups/` folder: the database (plants, comments, logins),
every photo, the guestbook and comments, the AI's saved answers, and a fingerprint of each piece. Its
name says the date, the time and who made it.

@menu screen-13-backups

1. **Back up now:** menu **6**, then **1** (a minute or two). Backups you make yourself are never
   deleted for you.
2. **Automatic backups** run by themselves: more often when the garden is busy, at least once a week
   (menu **6**, then **7** chooses automatic or every night).
3. **Keep three copies:** on the garden computer, on your NAS (**6**, then **4**) and on Google Drive
   (**6**, then **5**). A second NAS or a PC's shared folder: **6**, then **13**.
4. **Check the newest backup:** **6**, then **3** (or `bash garden.sh verify`).
5. **Restore:** menu **7**. Pick a backup from the list. The garden first makes a `before-restore`
   backup, so you can always go back.

Guide **13, Backups, restoring, and folder names** has all the details.

### 18.3 Settings (menu 8), people (U), keys (K) and more

@menu screen-10-settings

- **8) Settings:** your login name and email, the time zone, the email that sends password links, your
  password, who may use the garden tools, the web name, renaming a file or folder safely, the
  database's own passwords, and a local AI computer.
- **U) Users and access:** who can sign in; add a person who may edit, or a guest who may only look;
  remove a person; change a password; sign everybody out; and **LOCK DOWN** (only you, new passwords).
- **K) API keys:** paste, test or replace the keys for the free AI (Gemini, OpenRouter), the plant data
  (Perenual), Gmail and No-IP.
- **9) Parts:** switch the garden tools (/beds), the wiki and n8n on or off.
- **M) More:** the front door (HTTPS and your sign-in page), printing labels (**M**, then **19**),
  setting up the phone app (**M**, then **20**), the web address (**M**, then **16**), logs, rebuilding,
  and switching the whole garden off or on (**M**, then **14**).

Jun's Garden

Your website, your computer, your control. Read this page first. It takes 5 minutes.

What this is: the whole garden website (all your plants, photos, blog, wiki and garden tools) plus a menu program that installs it and helps you look after it. The menu explains every step before it happens, shows the exact commands, and saves everything in a logs folder you can read. Nothing gives anybody else access to your computer.

The full list of programs, with links to their official pages: WHAT-GETS-INSTALLED.md. Why each one was chosen: WHY-THESE-PROGRAMS.md.

Before you start

The whole path on one page: How it all works (a map of the install, Easy and Custom, and every feature).

No surprises: Before you start lists everything you will need, sorted into necessary and optional (sign-ups, keys, ports, the names and addresses to write down), with how long each part takes. It also explains Easy and Custom. Every guide starts with a short Quick version.

  • A computer that will be the garden server, and can stay switched on: an Ubuntu laptop (recommended), a Raspberry Pi, or a desktop. Guide 01 turns an old laptop into an Ubuntu computer.
  • Internet on that computer (a cable is the most reliable).
  • About 60 GB of free disk space on that computer (on Windows: about 60 GB free on C:).
  • About 1 to 2 hours. Most of it is waiting while the website builds (15 to 45 minutes; up to an hour on a very old laptop or a Pi).
  • Your password for that computer. Ubuntu asks for it now and then.
  • Later, not today: a free Gmail app password (for email) and a free No-IP account (to put the site on the internet). The menu explains both when you get there.

Your web name: keep it, or choose a new one

Your garden's address on the internet is a web name. It comes as junsgarden.zapto.org. At the start of the install you are asked once:

  • Keep junsgarden.zapto.org (recommended), or
  • Choose a new name (for example one for your mom's garden). The Website name swap puts the new name into every file that uses it. It leaves out https://, www. and anything after / for you, keeps a copy of each file it changes, and can be run again later (menu M, 16; on Windows: More, 17).

Getting a web name is free and takes about 5 minutes: an account at No-IP, then Dynamic DNS, No-IP Hostnames, Create Hostname. A free account has one name, and No-IP emails you a link to confirm it every 30 days (guide 04, Part A). You can choose the name now and add it at No-IP any time before going online.

Later, if you like: free DuckDNS names give other things their own address (for example Home Assistant panels or automations), each behind your own sign-in page and still through only the two router ports. Guide 04, "Advanced". No need to decide that today.

Install, step by step

Choose the computer you are reading this on:

1

Download the garden from Google Drive

Open the Google Drive link you were sent. The folder has three downloads:
FileWhatNeeded?
junsgarden-for-jun.zipYour plants, the handbook and the start files (about 5 MB). The program itself comes from GitHub the first time you startYes
junsgarden-photos-addon.zipAll the garden photos (about 800 MB)For the pictures. Optional to download by hand: the install fetches the photos itself (small parts from several places at once, unlocked with the key photos.key that is inside the main zip). Or put this zip in place yourself, now or later; you can get it again any time (menu 6, then 11)
junsgarden-wiki-addon.zipThe wiki's pages (under 1 MB)Only if you want the wiki
Download one file at a time: click one file, then Download (the arrow at the top). (If you choose several files at once, Drive packs them into one combined drive-download-...zip instead.) For a big file Google says it "can't scan this file for viruses": click Download anyway.
Where to keep them: they arrive in your Downloads folder. Open Files, go to Downloads, and drag each file onto Home in the left column.Open File Explorer, go to Downloads, and drag each file onto Desktop in the left column (if your Desktop is in OneDrive, that is fine). The installer finds the photos and wiki zips on the Desktop, or in Downloads if you leave them there. If the garden folder ends up inside OneDrive, the installer offers to move it out of OneDrive for you (Before you start, "If your Desktop is in OneDrive"). Leave the photos and wiki zips zipped: the install finds them, checks their fingerprints and puts everything together. Want a spare copy? The backups menu can keep the downloads on your NAS (menu 6, then 12).
2

Unpack (unzip) only the first one

Open Files, go to Home, right-click junsgarden-for-jun.zip and choose Extract Here. A new junsgarden-for-jun folder appears in Home.
On the Desktop, right-click junsgarden-for-jun.zip and choose Extract All. Windows suggests a folder named after the zip, which would give junsgarden-for-jun\junsgarden-for-jun. In the Extract window, remove the last part of the path so it ends in \Desktop, then click Extract. A new junsgarden-for-jun folder appears on the Desktop. (If you already extracted it the other way: open the junsgarden-for-jun folder inside it, and use that one.)
3

Start the menu

The first time, it gets the program itself from GitHub (about 75 MB, so it needs the internet) and checks every file against the list that came in the zip. That takes a minute or two, once. The zip from Google Drive holds only what is not on GitHub: your plants, the music, the handbook and the small start files.
Open the junsgarden-for-jun folder. Right-click Start-Here-Ubuntu.sh and choose Run as a Program. A terminal window opens with the menu.
No "Run as a Program"? Right-click an empty spot in the folder, choose Open in Terminal, then paste this (Ctrl+Shift+V pastes in a terminal) and press Enter:
bash Start-Here-Ubuntu.sh
Or open any terminal (Ctrl+Alt+T) and paste this one, which works from anywhere:
bash ~/junsgarden-for-jun/Start-Here-Ubuntu.sh
Open the extracted junsgarden-for-jun folder and double-click Start-Here-Windows.cmd. If Windows says "Windows protected your PC", click More info, then Run anyway (it is a plain text file you can open in Notepad to read first). The setup wizard opens: your steps on the left, Help, Back and Next below. First choose EASY (automatic, recommended) or CUSTOM (you choose, page by page: guide 23).
The garden always runs on Ubuntu. The installer asks where:
• Another computer at home (an Ubuntu laptop or a Raspberry Pi, recommended): you type its local IP address (on that computer type hostname -I; it looks like 192.168.1.50, or click Find it for me and pick it from the list) and your user name there. It copies everything over and installs it there. That computer needs the SSH server once:
sudo apt install -y openssh-server
• This Windows PC: Windows installs its own Ubuntu (WSL, by Microsoft) and the garden runs in it. Here the garden is for using on this PC itself. Putting it on the internet, and showing it to phones and other computers at home, needs a separate garden computer (an Ubuntu laptop or a Pi): Windows' own Ubuntu has its own internal address inside the PC that the router and your phones cannot reach. (To learn more: WSL networking, by Microsoft; the garden does not set this up.)
Then it asks which Docker runs the garden's programs: Docker Desktop, the easy way, with buttons to see, start and stop them (the installer gets it for you), or Docker inside Ubuntu, the hands-on way. Guide 26 compares them.

If you choose This Windows PC, the first time goes like this:

  • Windows asks permission to switch WSL on (click Yes), then needs one restart. After the restart, double-click Start-Here-Windows.cmd again and click through the same pages: it carries on.
  • Ubuntu for WSL then downloads (several hundred MB).
  • It asks you to choose an Ubuntu user name (for example jun) and a password, typed twice (nothing shows while you type). Write them down. It asks for that password again right after.
  • If WSL does not start after the restart, virtualization may need switching on in the PC's BIOS: Microsoft's WSL troubleshooting.
  • The PC must not go to sleep, or the garden stops: Settings, System, Power, Sleep = Never.
  • About 60 GB free on C: is needed.

The black window. On the Install page, Start the install opens a black window that does the real work and asks its questions there (EASY or CUSTOM is already chosen in the wizard). At the end, EASY asks: Login name [Jun] (just press Enter), then your new password (nothing shows while you type), then Set up email (Gmail) now, for 'forgot password' links? (y/N) (Enter means no; it then asks Are you sure? ... Skip email for now? (Y/n), and Enter skips it; menu 8, then 3 adds it later), then Add your Google Gemini key (AI Studio) now? (Y/n) (Enter means yes; at the paste prompt, Enter with nothing skips it; menu K adds it later), then the same question for an OpenRouter key (Enter means no). Keep the black window open until it says EASY install finished; then you can close it. The setup wizard's last page only appears after the password and keys are done.

For everyday jobs afterwards, double-click Garden-Menu-Windows.cmd (the same menu as on Ubuntu).
4

Type 1 and press Enter

The install asks where the garden should run (this computer, or another one by its IP address), then EASY or CUSTOM. Choose 1 EASY: everything the garden needs plus the garden tools, chosen for you; you only type your name, email and passwords. The wiki is not included: add it later only if you want it (menu 9, Parts), with its pages from the separate download junsgarden-wiki-addon.zip. (CUSTOM lets you choose every part; you can switch to it later by running the install again.) Then it shows every step in a list.
Want everything, the way the garden runs today? Guide 23, the full setup.
A step that is already installed and working is skipped, and if anything stops, running it again carries on. Each step explains what it installs, why, and where to read more, then shows its commands in blue before running them. Answer the questions with y or n (or just press Enter for the suggested answer in capitals).
When Ubuntu asks for a password, nothing shows while you type. That is normal: type it and press Enter.
How long: building the website is the longest step: 15 to 45 minutes (up to an hour on a very old laptop or a Pi). After the long build it may ask for your Ubuntu password again; nothing happens until you type it.
Right after a fresh Ubuntu install, Ubuntu updates itself for 10 to 30 minutes. If step 3 says it cannot install, wait 15 minutes and choose 1 again.
5

At the end: your password, then open your garden

EASY ends with: Login name [Jun] (your name in brackets: just press Enter), then your new password (nothing shows while you type), then Set up email (Gmail) now, for 'forgot password' links? (y/N) (Enter means no; it then asks Are you sure? ... Skip email for now? (Y/n), and Enter skips it; menu 8, then 3 adds it later), then Add your Google Gemini key (AI Studio) now? (Y/n) (Enter means yes; at the paste prompt, Enter with nothing skips it; menu K adds it later), then the same question for an OpenRouter key (Enter means no). Keep the window open until it says EASY install finished; then you can close it.

CUSTOM instead ends with your password (do this one), your AI keys, email and reserving the computer's address in the router (from the setup wizard, it uses the answers you typed there).

Then open http://localhost:8030 on the garden computer (from Windows with a separate garden computer: the Windows menu 4). Your plants and photos are already there.

What gets installed

Each program runs in its own small box (Docker). Why boxes, and not whole pretend computers? What is Docker? explains it with two pictures, in plain words. Just for your information. Every program, its version and its maker's documents: guide 21.

Necessary

Ubuntu updates, firewall, SSH server, Docker, the database (MariaDB), your website, your plants and photos, automatic backups.

Optional (it asks)

Garden tools (/beds), the wiki, email with your Gmail, a desktop icon.

Advanced (later, menu M)

The front door: HTTPS (Caddy), your sign-in page (Authelia), your web name (No-IP), router ports 80 and 443. Also n8n, the database viewer, local AI, Tailscale (reach it from anywhere), your own names for folders and pages, and the on / off switch (guide 26).

If something stops or looks stuck

  • Nothing is lost. Start the menu again and choose 1. It shows which steps are done and carries on where it stopped. In CUSTOM you can also type another step number.
  • The file logs/install-progress.txt shows every step with the time it started and finished.
  • Menu 5: the checklist says PASS, WARN or FAIL for everything, and what to do about each FAIL.
  • Menu H makes a help file (no passwords in it) to paste into Gemini or show to someone helping you.
  • The building step can look frozen for several minutes on an old laptop. If text is still slowly appearing, it is working.
  • More fixes: START-HERE/09-troubleshooting.md.

Putting the garden on the internet (later)

When the garden works at home, menu M then 1 (the front door) does it. Its first choice, "Not on the internet yet", shows the garden to phones and computers on your home Wi-Fi only. When you are ready for the internet (www), choice 3 also installs the No-IP updater (DUC), which keeps your web name pointing at the garden computer.

Garden inside Windows' own Ubuntu (WSL) on a Windows PC? Then this does not work as written: Windows' own Ubuntu has its own internal address inside the PC that the router and your phones cannot reach. On that PC the garden is for using on the PC itself. Putting the garden online, and showing it to other devices at home, needs a separate garden computer (an Ubuntu laptop or a Pi). To learn more: WSL networking, by Microsoft (the garden does not set this up).

You will need:

  • Your own free No-IP account with your web name (junsgarden.zapto.org, or the one you chose), and a DDNS key for it (guide 04 shows the clicks).
  • The garden computer's address reserved in your router (guide 14).
  • In the router: forward only TCP ports 80 and 443 to that one address. Nothing else.

You can practise first with a made-up name ending in .test: everything works on the garden computer only, and nothing on the internet changes.

Everyday use

Open the menu (the Jun's Garden icon, or the steps above), then:

  • 2 start, 3 stop, 4 open the website
  • 5 checklist, 6 backups (also to your NAS and Google Drive), 7 restore a backup
  • 8 Settings: change your name, email, passwords or folders, with no files to edit
  • 9 Parts: switch the garden tools, wiki or n8n on or off
  • U Users: add people or guests, remove people, lock everything down to just you
  • K API keys: paste, test or replace your Gemini, Gmail and other keys (never shown on screen)
  • L Language: English, 한국어, or both (what you type stays the same)
  • H Help file: what happened, with no passwords, to show someone helping you

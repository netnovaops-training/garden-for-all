How it all works

One page with the whole path: from the download to a running garden, and every feature after that. Each box has the same colour as its folder of PDFs ("PDF guides"), so you can find the full guide quickly. The installer explains every step on screen too. If you are ever unsure where you are, come back to this page.

0Get ready

  • Read Before you start: what you need, what is necessary and what is optional, and how long each part takes.
  • Download from Google Drive: junsgarden-for-jun.zip (needed), junsgarden-photos-addon.zip (the photos; optional by hand, the install can fetch them itself), junsgarden-wiki-addon.zip (only for the wiki).
  • Keep them on the Desktop (Windows) or in your Home folder (Ubuntu). Unzip only the first one. The first start then gets the program itself from GitHub (about 75 MB, the internet is needed once) and checks every file.
↓

1The garden computer

  • A separate Ubuntu laptop or a Raspberry Pi (recommended; needed to put the garden online): guide 01 puts Ubuntu on it. This is the only part the installer cannot do for you.
  • Or this Windows PC: the installer adds Windows' own Ubuntu (WSL). Good for using the garden on this PC. One Windows restart the first time.
↓

2Start the install

On Windows

Double-click Start-Here-Windows.cmd. Garden Bridge opens: five pages with Next:
  1. Welcome (and this map)
  2. Where the garden runs
  3. About you, and your web name
  4. What gets installed (tick the parts)
  5. Install: a black window opens and does the work

On Ubuntu

Right-click Start-Here-Ubuntu.sh, Run as a Program. The menu opens. Type 1 and Enter.

Then it asks once: EASY or CUSTOM.

1) EASY (recommended)

  • Asks only your name, email, the database password (Enter makes one), and whether to keep the web name.
  • Installs everything necessary plus the garden tools.
  • Loads your photos by itself.
  • Wiki and n8n: off (add them later).

2) CUSTOM

  • The same questions, plus each optional part, one by one.
  • You choose: photos now or later; wiki yes or no; n8n yes or no.
  • You can start at any step number.
↓

2The 14 steps

Each step says what it does, why, where to read more, and how long it usually takes; afterwards it says how long it took. Stop any time: choose 1 again and it carries on.

StepsWhat happensUsually
1 to 2Checks the computer and the folderunder a minute
3 to 7Updates, SSH, firewall, Docker, swap (on small computers)10 to 25 minutes
8 to 11Settings file, parts, folders, change historyabout a minute
12Builds the website, loads your plants and photos, starts everything15 to 45 minutes (up to an hour on a very old laptop or a Pi)
13 to 14Backup schedule, desktop iconunder a minute

At the end: EASY asks your login name (just Enter), then your password (nothing shows while you type), then your AI keys (Enter with nothing skips). CUSTOM offers the password, email and reserving the address. It may ask for your Ubuntu password again after the long build.

3Later, only if you want: going online

  • Menu M, 1 (the front door): 1) home Wi-Fi only, 2) practice, 3) your real web name with the padlock.
  • Needs a free No-IP name and the router: guide 04, guide 14. Only ports 80 and 443, ever.
  • Extra addresses for other things (DuckDNS subdomains), working from another room (SSH, Remote Desktop: guide 20).
↓

4Later: email, keys and AI

  • A Gmail app password, so "forgot password" emails reach you (menu M, 3).
  • Your own free AI keys (menu K): guide 16. Local AI on a desktop: guide 15. The phone app: guide 17.
↓

5Later: three copies of everything

  • Backups are automatic: more often when the garden is busy, at least once a week (menu 6, then 7). Add a NAS and Google Drive (menu 6): guide 13.

If you are stuck

  1. Nothing is lost. Choose 1 again: it shows which steps are done and carries on.
  2. Menu 5, the checklist, says what is wrong and what to do.
  3. Guide 09: fixes by what you see.
  4. Menu H makes a help file (no passwords) to show a friend or an AI.
